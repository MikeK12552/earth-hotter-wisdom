# Standing watchlist

A starting point for the weekly role check, not current fact. The prompt requires every
role to be verified fresh each week. When you verify or correct something here, edit
this file in the same commit and change the date in brackets. When a correction
contradicts an argument written in this file, rewrite the argument rather than leaving
it standing next to the corrected fact.

## Institutional agenda-setters

**Erin Sikorsky** *(verified 2026-09-20)* — Director, Center for Climate and Security and
the International Military Council on Climate and Security. Both directorships re-confirmed
this week against councilonstrategicrisks.org/ccs/, which also gives **Tom Ellison** as
deputy director of CCS, **John Conger** as director emeritus, and **Noah Fritzhand** and
**Katherine Yusko** as research fellows, alongside roughly twenty-five non-resident
fellows. Previously Deputy Director
of the Strategic Futures Group at the US National Intelligence Council, where she led
the intelligence community's climate analysis and co-authored *Global Trends*; founding
chair of the Congressionally mandated Climate Security Advisory Council. Writes *Hot
Fronts: Security in a Warming World*; contributing editor at Lawfare. The most useful
person for the US trajectory, especially its reversals. Her May 2026 turn to solar
radiation modification governance, with Shuchi Talati in Lawfare, is an early indicator
of where the security frame travels next.

*Correction, 2026-08-31:* an earlier version of this file called her March 2026 piece on
climate's omission from the 2026 US Annual Threat Assessment "the mirror image of the
2007 CNA moment". It is not. CNA 2007 was supply-side: retired flag officers
manufacturing authority that did not exist and handing policymakers a vocabulary. A
think-tank critique of an omission is demand-side, into a space that has already closed.
One created an agenda; the other documents the removal of one. Do not carry the metaphor
forward; it overstates the critique's leverage.

*Added 2026-09-20:* the more useful thing to track about this institute is now **who it
writes for**. Its most regular climate-security product is a state-level briefer series —
South Carolina (May 2025), Colorado, Hawaiʻi and Washington (December 2025), Ohio
(18 September 2026, by Madelyn MacMurray and Caroline Mallory) — addressing extreme
weather threats to installations, infrastructure and local economies. That is the
disaster-operations register with a statehouse reader, and it is what an institute does
when its federal security customer closes. Two questions to work each week: whether the
series continues and adds states with large installations, and whether any state
government is ever named as commissioner or partner. No client is stated on any of the
five. A second US institute writing for statehouses would make this a field-level shift
rather than one organisation's strategy.

*Updated 2026-10-04, and the state-level reading needs demoting.* No new state briefer has
appeared since the Ohio one of 18 September — two consecutive weeks — while the institute's
only climate item between 28 September and 4 October was the announcement, on 2 October, of
a **private tabletop exercise with Reflective** on the security dynamics of a fictional
2030 **stratospheric aerosol injection** deployment to slow Arctic warming, scheduled for
14 October alongside the Montréal Climate Security Summit, with no participants named and
no output document. Three registers in three weeks — statehouse briefers, an analysis of
NATO's new resilience requirements, now a great-power geoengineering scenario — which reads
as several simultaneous bets rather than a relocation. **Treat the statehouse series as one
bet among several, not as the institute's strategy.**

The SRM indicator flagged above has landed, and the format is the finding. A tabletop
produces no citable document, has no named participants and leaves nothing anyone can
dispute, which makes it the instrument of choice for a subject too radioactive to publish
on. Whether it surfaces risks or normalises the premise cannot be determined from outside
and will not be determinable afterwards either. Two things to watch after 16 October:
whether anything citable emerges, and whether any state or alliance body is revealed to
have taken part. And one caution on reading the scenario's own framing, which concerns how
different deployment designs would affect sea ice: that is a question of physical efficacy
that a geopolitical tabletop cannot answer and in which the co-convening research
organisation has a direct interest.

supports. The rhetoric is the field's own, the analysis is competent, and the resource
commitment is a rounding error; all three are true and the field will report the first
two. The standing question here is whether the proportion is a one-off or the going rate.

**Johann Wadephul** *(verified 2026-10-11)* — **German Federal Foreign Minister**, in
office since May 2025, confirmed against his ministry's own publication of his speech
opening the **eighth Berlin Climate and Security Conference** on 6 October 2026. Added
because he said the newest thing anyone said this week, and it is a departure from the
field's own vocabulary: actors are **using the impacts of climate change as a weapon**, and
doing so is perfidious. Note what that formulation does. *Threat multiplier* names a
condition and assigns no agency; *weaponisation* supplies an adversary. The published text
names no actor as the weaponiser, which is why this is positioning rather than a finding —
but it is the first time in this routine's run that the climate-security argument has
acquired a perpetrator, and it is the register in which a German foreign ministry can speak
to a coalition that does not lead with climate.

The rest of the speech is supply chains rather than conflict data: drought in Sudan and
falling Nile levels, reduced Panama Canal transits cutting daily traffic and vessel draft,
El Niño threatening rice, palm oil, coffee and maize harvests in Asia, the Strait of Hormuz
situation feeding into fertiliser supply and energy prices — and Sudan and the Panama Canal
12,000 km apart, which is the point he is making. None of that chain is an attributed
finding and the Hormuz link is not a climate link at all; expect it to be cited as though
both were. He also said climate mitigation investment is security investment, and that
decentralised renewables could be vital to survival if Russia strikes Ukraine's energy
infrastructure again this winter.

**Three things to track from him.** First, the **Group of Friends on Climate and Security
at 81 members**, co-chaired with Nauru — the only countable institutional indicator this
field produced in the week, in either direction, and worth re-reading each time he speaks
because a membership number is a trend line. It binds nobody and spends nothing. Second,
**what he put his ministry's name to, which is all pre-existing and all small**: the UN
Climate Security Mechanism (US$18.27m in nine years, Germany a contributor and
co-founder), the **Nairobi Toolkit** with Kenya inside the **Global Counterterrorism
Forum**, the **Weathering Risk** initiative, **CERF's Climate Action Account**, and
cross-border adaptation-and-peacebuilding work in Ethiopia, Kenya and Somalia. No new money
and no new machinery was announced. Third, **climate-resilient reconstruction in Syria**,
discussed with Syrian representatives present — the first instance this routine has
recorded of climate adaptation entering a post-conflict reconstruction agenda at
ministerial level, and the sort of unglamorous item that becomes durable practice.
**And the standing question:** whether any *defence* ministry, German or otherwise, ever
repeats the weaponisation claim. Nothing was found this week, including from his own
government's.

*Added 2026-10-11, on the Council of the European Union as a tracked actor in its own
right, not only via the Commission and the EEAS.* A **draft of the Union's negotiating
position for COP31**, reported on 6 October from a copy seen by Bloomberg, says there is
**"no scope"** for new climate finance commitments at the conference, emphasises
implementing existing decisions, seeks the **Mitigation Work Programme** extended past its
COP31 expiry to **at least 2040**, and seeks the 2028 Global Stocktake set up. Environment
ministers were to approve conclusions on the Union's negotiating positions for COP31 and
COP17 in **Luxembourg on 12 October**, alongside the proposed ETS review. **Read it with
the resilience framework of 28 October, not alone.** An instrument built on a 3°C reference
trajectory, named risk owners, member-state resilience plans conditioning access to Union
funding, and an insurance alliance, launched in the same month as a pre-emptive refusal of
new international finance, describes a bloc managing climate risk by **domestic allocation
and insurance** rather than by international transfer. That is the clearest answer this
routine has to the standing question of which register European climate policy is written
in: not security, not solidarity, but risk management. **Two things to check:** whether the
phrase survives into the adopted conclusions, and whether the Mitigation Work Programme
extension is what it is traded for. Note also the agenda separation visible in the Council's
own 9 October forward look — the **European Council of 15–16 October** carries European
defence and security with no climate item, while climate sits in a separate council three
days earlier. Agenda separation is a weaker observable than a deleted referent; record it as
the weaker thing.

***Corrected again 2026-10-11, and this one is the important correction on this page.***
The instruction added a week earlier — "ask about the certifier, not the number" — was also
wrong, and wrong in the same way: it adopted a sympathetic actor's framing as a description
of the mechanism. **Nobody is proposing a substitute certifier.** No state tabled an
alternative scientific body in any forum. What is contested is the **date**: five
consecutive IPCC plenaries have failed to decide whether AR7's working-group reports land
before the second global stocktake closes in 2028 or in 2029, four proposals remain, and
the stocktake's political phase ends in 2028, so a 2029 report cannot inform it. And the
states pressing for the later timetable — China, India, Kenya, Russia and Saudi Arabia per
reporting of the plenaries — give **developing-country review and approval capacity** as
their ground, which is the same register as the small-state objection above. One
constituency invokes capacity to keep the panel central; another invokes it to slow the
panel down. **Ask about the calendar, not the certifier.** And when a negotiator supplies a
quotable formulation, establish what it is a formulation of before building a thread on it.
Michael's sentence about scientific bodies remains worth quoting as an argument about
participation; it is not a description of what is happening.

So the rule needs splitting, and the split is about whether the institution has published
a schedule:

- **Silence from a body with a standing publication cycle is evidence**, because the
  schedule is the commitment. NATO's missed Climate Change and Security Impact
  Assessments are the type case: an annual instrument that ran 2022, 2023 and 2024 and
  has now missed two cycles without any statement tells you something about NATO's
  priorities. Keep scoring that every week.

  long the work takes. The question to ask of an attribution network is not "did it
  publish this week" but **"has it declined a case it was asked to take"** — a stated
  refusal, or a published case selection that passes over an event. Absence of output is
  not a refusal.

apply to the Forum Secretariat, which has an annual leaders' meeting and communiqué and
therefore belongs in the first category.

carried into 2026-09-13 and the study refutes it: what WWA declined was the **event-level

have occurred in the absence of human-induced climate change"), while attributing the
hazard environment — roughly +1.5°C on July–August temperatures, about +2°C annually, the
0°C isotherm rising about 100 m per decade — and concluding the event exceeded the limits
of existing risk reduction and early warning. The method exists for this hazard class; it
