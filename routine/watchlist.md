# Standing watchlist

A starting point for the weekly role check, not current fact. The prompt requires every
role to be verified fresh each week. When you verify or correct something here, edit
this file in the same commit and change the date in brackets. When a correction
contradicts an argument written in this file, rewrite the argument rather than leaving
it standing next to the corrected fact.

## Institutional agenda-setters

**Erin Sikorsky** *(verified Aug 2026)* — Director, Center for Climate and Security and
the International Military Council on Climate and Security. Previously Deputy Director
of the Strategic Futures Group at the US National Intelligence Council, where she led
the intelligence community's climate analysis and co-authored *Global Trends*; founding
chair of the Congressionally mandated Climate Security Advisory Council. Writes *Hot
Fronts: Security in a Warming World*; contributing editor at Lawfare. The most useful
person for the US trajectory, especially its reversals. Her May 2026 turn to solar
radiation modification governance, with Shuchi Talati in Lawfare, is an early indicator
of where the security frame travels next.

*Correction, 2026-08-31:* an earlier version of this file called her March 2026 piece on
climate's omission from the 2026 US Annual Threat Assessment "the mirror image of the
2007 CNA moment". It is not. CNA 2007 was supply-side: retired flag officers
manufacturing authority that did not exist and handing policymakers a vocabulary. A
think-tank critique of an omission is demand-side, into a space that has already closed.
One created an agenda; the other documents the removal of one. Do not carry the metaphor
forward; it overstates the critique's leverage.

**Louise van Schaik** *(verified Aug 2026)* — Clingendael: Head of Unit EU & Global
Affairs, Programme Lead Critical Resources, project lead of the Planetary Security
Initiative; visiting professor at the College of Europe. Current statement: *The
Resilience Agenda*, with Hannah Lentschig (Systemiq with the University of Oxford and
Clingendael, 5 February 2026).

**Wopke Hoekstra** *(verified Aug 2026)* — European Commissioner for Climate, Net Zero
and Clean Growth since October 2023, continuing into the second von der Leyen
Commission. The test case for whether the security framing survives contact with
competitiveness politics: he bundles climate, energy security and strategic independence
into a single argument, which is either the frame's greatest institutional success or
its dilution into industrial policy.

**Simon Stiell** *(verified Aug 2026)* — UNFCCC Executive Secretary since August 2022,
reappointed for a second three-year term in June 2025. Watch how much security language
he does and does not use; the secretariat has structural reasons for caution.

**The UN Secretary-General succession** *(verified Aug 2026)* — António Guterres's term
ends 31 December 2026. What the candidates say about climate, and about Security Council
competence over it, is among the highest-value things to track this year.

**Annalena Baerbock** *(verified Aug 2026)* — German Foreign Minister December 2021 to
May 2025, succeeded by Johann Wadephul; President of the 80th UN General Assembly since
9 September 2025, term ending 8 September 2026, then a professorship at Columbia's
School of International and Public Affairs. Her ministry's climate foreign policy
strategy is now a legacy document under a different government; whether it survived the
transition is the analytically interesting question.

**Tom Middendorp** *(unverified)* — former Chief of Defence of the Netherlands, IMCCS
chair, senior research associate at Clingendael. The Dutch instance of a recurring
pattern: a retired flag officer carrying credibility into a defence ministry that a
civilian researcher cannot.

**Sherri Goodman** *(unverified)* — widely credited with originating "threat multiplier"
as US Deputy Under Secretary of Defense (Environmental Security) and later at CNA;
author of a 2024 memoir of that title. Verify the coinage attribution before repeating
it.

**To add:** the head of the UN Climate Security Mechanism, NATO's climate and security
officials, whoever holds the EEAS climate-security file, the AOSIS chair, the Pacific
Islands Forum Secretary-General, and the equivalent figures in the African Union, Indian
and Brazilian systems.

## Academics whose positions set the terms of debate

- **Halvard Buhaug** (PRIO) — the most consistent methodological sceptic on
  climate-conflict claims. If he changes his mind about something, that is itself a move.
- **Katharine Mach** (University of Miami) — led the 2019 *Nature* expert elicitation;
  the closest thing the field has to a consensus broker.
- **Solomon Hsiang** and **Marshall Burke** (Stanford) — the quantitative programme.
- **Jan Selby** (Leeds) — the Syria rebuttal and the broader critique of policy
  narratives outrunning evidence.
- **Marwa Daoudy** (Georgetown), **Tor Benjaminsen** (NMBU), **Farhana Sultana**
  (Syracuse), **Simon Dalby** — political ecology, regional expertise and the critical
  strand; the defence against absorbing the advocacy line.
- **Florian Krampe** (SIPRI) and **Cedric de Coning** (NUPI) *(unverified)* — climate and
  peacebuilding practice, where research meets mandate design.
- **Beatrice Mosello** and colleagues at adelphi *(unverified)* — the European
  policy-research interface.
- **New entrants** from adjacent fields: critical minerals economists, migration
  demographers, SRM governance scholars. They change the vocabulary faster than
  incumbents.
